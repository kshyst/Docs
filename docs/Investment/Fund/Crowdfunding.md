# Crowdfunding

Crowdfunding is a method of raising capital for a project, business, or cause by collecting small amounts of money from a large number of people, typically via the internet. It bypasses traditional financial intermediaries like banks or venture capitalists, allowing creators to pitch directly to the public.

---

## 1. The Four Major Models of Crowdfunding

Depending on what backers receive in exchange for their funds, crowdfunding is categorized into four main models:

| Model | Description | Primary Reward | Prominent Platforms |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rewards-Based** | Backers pledge money in exchange for a non-monetary reward, often the product itself. | Pre-orders, early access, merchandise | Kickstarter, Indiegogo |
| **Donation-Based** | Backers donate money to support a charitable cause, personal emergency, or social project without expecting any return. | Altruistic satisfaction | GoFundMe, JustGiving |
| **Debt-Based (P2P)** | Also known as Peer-to-Peer lending. Backers lend money to an individual or business with the expectation of being repaid with interest. | Principal repayment + interest | LendingClub, Kiva, Prosper |
| **Equity-Based** | Backers invest in early-stage private companies in exchange for an ownership stake (shares). | Equity shares, future dividends, or exit returns | Wefunder, StartEngine, SeedInvest |

## 2. Equity Crowdfunding Deep Dive

Equity crowdfunding democratizes startup investing, allowing everyday individuals to act as angel investors.

### Valuations: Pre-Money vs. Post-Money
When a startup raises money, it sets a valuation that determines how much equity investors receive:
* **Pre-Money Valuation**: The agreed-upon value of the startup before it receives the new investment round.
* **Post-Money Valuation**: The value of the company *after* the investment is added. 
  $$\text{Post-Money Valuation} = \text{Pre-Money Valuation} + \text{Investment Raised}$$
* *Example*: If a startup has a pre-money valuation of \$4M and raises \$1M, its post-money valuation is \$5M. The new investors collectively own 20% (\$1M / \$5M) of the company.

### Dilution
Dilution occurs when a company issues new shares in subsequent funding rounds. While the total number of shares increases, the percentage of ownership held by early investors decreases. However, if the company's valuation grows, the smaller percentage may be worth significantly more than the original larger percentage.

### Exit Strategies
Unlike public stocks, startup shares are highly illiquid. Investors typically cannot sell their shares until a liquidity event (an "exit") occurs:
1. **Initial Public Offering (IPO)**: The company lists its shares on a public stock exchange.
2. **Acquisition**: A larger company buys the startup, converting the shares to cash or stock in the acquiring company.
3. **Secondary Markets**: Specialized platforms that facilitate the trading of private shares prior to an IPO (subject to restrictions).

## 3. Regulatory Framework (SEC Regulation CF)

In the United States, equity crowdfunding is regulated under the **JOBS Act of 2012**, specifically through **Regulation Crowdfunding (Reg CF)** overseen by the SEC.

### Regulation Crowdfunding (Reg CF)
* **Funding Cap**: Startups can raise a maximum of **\$5 million** in a 12-month period through Reg CF.
* **Funding Portals**: All Reg CF campaigns must be hosted online through an SEC-registered and FINRA-member broker-dealer or funding portal (e.g., Wefunder, StartEngine).

### Accredited vs. Non-Accredited Investors
The SEC categorizes investors to protect less experienced individuals from financial ruin:
* **Accredited Investors**: Individuals with an annual income exceeding \$200,000 (or \$300,000 joint) for the past two years, or a net worth exceeding \$1 million (excluding their primary residence). There are no limits on how much they can invest.
* **Non-Accredited Investors**: Everyday investors. The amount they can invest in all Reg CF offerings over a 12-month period is capped based on a percentage of their annual income and net worth.

### Disclosure Requirements
Startups raising capital must file a **Form C** with the SEC, disclosing:
* Progress updates and how the raised capital will be used.
* Detailed information about founders, officers, and owners of 20% or more of the company.
* Financial statements: Depending on the target raise, these may need to be reviewed by an independent CPA or fully audited.

## 4. Pros and Cons of Equity Crowdfunding

### For Startups / Founders
* **Pros**:
  * **Access to Capital**: Opens funding to businesses that might be rejected by traditional VCs or banks.
  * **Marketing & Validation**: Validates product-market fit and builds a community of loyal, brand-advocate shareholders.
* **Cons**:
  * **Dilution & Cap Table Management**: Managing hundreds or thousands of micro-shareholders can be legally complex.
  * **Administrative Overhead**: Ongoing SEC filing and financial disclosure requirements.

### For Investors
* **Pros**:
  * **Democratization**: Access to high-growth startup deals previously restricted to VCs.
  * **High Potential Returns**: A small investment in a successful startup can yield exponential returns.
* **Cons**:
  * **Extreme Risk of Loss**: The majority of early-stage startups fail, resulting in a 100% loss of capital.
  * **Illiquidity**: Money can be locked up for 5 to 10+ years with no guarantee of an exit.
